# The Sleep-Wake Continuum Hypothesis of Psychedelic Action: A Theoretical Framework and Call for Empirical Testing

**Target Journal:** Neuroscience of Consciousness

---

## Abstract

We propose a novel theoretical framework: different psychedelics may induce distinct positions along a sleep-wake continuum, producing hybrid states that blend waking consciousness with sleep-like neural signatures. Reviewing EEG/MEG studies of psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, ayahuasca, and mescaline, we find patterns *consistent with* but not *confirmatory of* this hypothesis. DMT uniquely shows theta/delta emergence at peak effects (n=13), resembling REM sleep signatures. 5-MeO-DMT shows delta dominance in rodent studies, resembling NREM/slow-wave sleep. Psilocybin and LSD show broadband desynchronization without theta emergence, suggesting enhanced waking rather than sleep-state invasion. However, critical limitations constrain interpretation: sample sizes are small (n=13-20), no head-to-head comparisons exist, ayahuasca (containing DMT) shows theta *decrease* rather than increase, and the proposed REM-Similarity Index remains untested. We present this framework not as established fact but as a falsifiable hypothesis generating specific predictions: (1) theta emergence should correlate with dream-like phenomenology across compounds (r > 0.5), (2) 5-MeO-DMT should show NREM-like signatures in adequately powered human studies, (3) acute EEG signatures may predict therapeutic outcomes. We detail the studies needed to test—and potentially falsify—this framework.

**Keywords:** psychedelics, EEG, sleep, REM, theta oscillations, DMT, psilocybin, consciousness, hypothesis

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Problem: Why Do Different Psychedelics Feel Different?

Despite sharing 5-HT2A agonism as a primary mechanism, classic psychedelics produce qualitatively distinct experiences. DMT users report immersive visual worlds with entity encounters. 5-MeO-DMT produces formless dissolution without imagery. Psilocybin and LSD tend toward conceptual insights and perceptual enhancement while maintaining reality contact. Current theoretical models do not adequately explain these differences.

### 1.2 Current Models and Their Limitations

**The Entropic Brain Hypothesis** (Carhart-Harris et al., 2014) proposes that psychedelics increase neural entropy. While supported by signal diversity measures, this model predicts similar experiences across compounds—the opposite of what users report.

**REBUS** (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019) elegantly explains insight and belief revision but again does not differentiate between compounds.

**Receptor-based models** cannot explain why structurally similar compounds (DMT vs. 5-MeO-DMT) produce opposite phenomenologies.

### 1.3 The Proposal: A Sleep-Wake Continuum

We propose that psychedelics induce varying degrees of **sleep-state invasion into waking consciousness**. This is not a claim that psychedelics *are* sleep states, but that their neural signatures may *resemble* sleep states to varying degrees, potentially explaining phenomenological differences.

**This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.** We present the existing evidence, acknowledge its limitations, and specify what would falsify the framework.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Literature Search

We searched PubMed and Google Scholar for EEG/MEG studies of classic psychedelics, extracting power spectral data by frequency band where reported.

### 2.2 Evidence Quality Rating

We rate evidence quality for each claim:
- **Strong**: Multiple studies, n > 20, replicated findings
- **Moderate**: Single well-controlled study, n = 15-20
- **Weak**: Single small study (n < 15) or animal models
- **Speculative**: No direct evidence; extrapolated from other findings

### 2.3 REM-Similarity Index (Theoretical)

We propose—but do not validate—a composite metric combining theta/alpha ratio, delta suppression, alpha suppression, and signal diversity. **This index has not been calculated from actual data or tested as a predictor.** Values presented are rough estimates for illustrative purposes only.

## 3. Results: What the Data Actually Show

### 3.1 DMT: The Strongest Evidence for Sleep-State Invasion

**Evidence Quality: MODERATE**

Timmermann et al. (2019) administered IV DMT to 13 participants (placebo-controlled, within-subjects):

| Finding | Direction | Confidence |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Alpha/beta power decrease | Strong decrease | High |
| Delta/theta emergence at peak | Increase in oscillatory component | Moderate |
| Signal diversity increase | Maintained high | High |
| Correlation with visuals | "Correlated" (r not reported) | Low |

**Interpretation**: DMT produces a pattern resembling REM sleep: theta emergence, alpha suppression, maintained signal diversity. The authors noted this resembles "functional deafferentation" from external input.

**Limitations**: n=13; correlation statistics not fully reported; single study.

### 3.2 5-MeO-DMT: Suggestive but Mainly Rodent Data

**Evidence Quality: WEAK**

Martial et al. (2024) recorded LFP in rats after 5-MeO-DMT:

| Finding | Direction | Confidence |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Delta power increase | Strong increase | Moderate (rodents) |
| Theta power decrease | Decrease | Moderate (rodents) |
| Gamma suppression | Strong decrease | Moderate (rodents) |

One human study (2025, n=29) confirmed delta dominance but lacks phenomenology correlations.

**Interpretation**: Pattern resembles NREM/slow-wave sleep. Authors termed it "paradoxical wakefulness."

**Limitations**: Primarily rodent data; human replication limited; translation uncertain.

### 3.3 Psilocybin: Broadband Desynchronization

**Evidence Quality: MODERATE**

Muthukumaraswamy et al. (2013), n=15, MEG:

| Finding | Direction | Confidence |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Broadband power reduction | Decrease all bands < 50 Hz | High |
| Alpha suppression | Strong decrease | High |
| Theta emergence | **None observed** | High |
| Signal diversity | Increased | High |

**Interpretation**: Enhanced waking, not sleep-state invasion. Desynchronization without theta/delta emergence.

**Important**: Skosnik et al. (2023) found theta *increased* 2 weeks post-psilocybin, correlating with depression improvement. This likely reflects neuroplasticity, not acute state effects.

### 3.4 LSD: Similar to Psilocybin

**Evidence Quality: MODERATE**

Carhart-Harris et al. (2016), n=20; Pallavicini et al. (2019):

| Finding | Direction | Confidence |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Alpha suppression | Strong decrease | High |
| Theta decrease | Decrease (not emergence) | Moderate |
| Signal diversity | Increased | High |

**Interpretation**: Wake-like pattern; no evidence of sleep-state invasion.

### 3.5 Ayahuasca: THE CRITICAL ANOMALY

**Evidence Quality: MODERATE (but contradicts hypothesis)**

Riba et al. (2002), n=18:

| Finding | Direction | Confidence |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Theta decrease | **Strong decrease** | High |
| Delta decrease | Decrease | Moderate |
| Beta/gamma increase | Increase | Moderate |

**This is a serious problem for the hypothesis.** Ayahuasca contains DMT. If DMT causes theta emergence (the core REM-like signature), ayahuasca should show theta emergence. **It shows the opposite.**

**Possible explanations** (none fully satisfactory):
1. MAOIs alter the signature
2. Oral vs. IV administration produces different dynamics
3. Extended duration changes the pattern
4. The classification is wrong

**We flag this as a potential falsification of the simple framework.**

### 3.6 Mescaline: Insufficient Modern Data

**Evidence Quality: WEAK**

Only historical studies (1950s-60s) and rodent data. Classification uncertain.

## 5. The Proposed Framework (Tentative)

**If** the patterns hold in adequately powered studies, psychedelics might be classified as:

| Class | Compounds | EEG Pattern | Phenomenology |
|-------|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| REM-like | DMT (IV) | Theta emergence, alpha collapse | Immersive visuals, entities |
| NREM-like | 5-MeO-DMT | Delta dominance, theta suppressed | Formless dissolution |
| Wake-like | Psilocybin, LSD | Broadband desync, no theta emergence | Insights, perceptual changes |
| **Unclassified** | Ayahuasca | Theta decrease (anomalous) | Mixed |

**This classification is theoretical.** It has not been derived from unsupervised clustering or validated by any predictive test.

## 6. What Would Falsify This Hypothesis?

We commit to specific falsification criteria:

### Falsified if:
1. **Theta emergence does NOT correlate with dream-like phenomenology** in multi-compound study (r < 0.2 would falsify; r > 0.5 would support)

2. **5-MeO-DMT shows theta emergence in humans** (would contradict NREM-like classification)

3. **High-dose psilocybin shows theta emergence** (would suggest dose, not compound, determines classification)

4. **Ayahuasca anomaly cannot be explained** (already partially falsifying)

5. **Unsupervised clustering groups compounds by chemistry, not EEG profile** (simpler explanation would suffice)

### Supported if:
1. Head-to-head study confirms differential theta/delta patterns
2. RSI calculated from data predicts phenomenology
3. Acute EEG predicts therapeutic outcomes
4. Individual differences in RSI explain outcome variance

---

## 7. Required Studies

### Priority 1: Head-to-Head Comparison (CRITICAL)

**Design**: Within-subjects, n=30, comparing IV DMT, oral psilocybin, oral LSD with identical EEG methods

**Why essential**: All current evidence comes from separate studies with different methods, populations, and analyses. Without direct comparison, classification is confounded.

### Priority 2: Acute EEG → Outcomes

**Design**: Psilocybin for depression (n=60), with acute EEG and 12-week follow-up

**Why essential**: Tests clinical utility of framework

### Priority 3: 5-MeO-DMT Human Replication

**Design**: High-density EEG during 5-MeO-DMT with phenomenology measures (n=25)

**Why essential**: Current NREM-like classification based primarily on rodents

### Priority 4: Ayahuasca vs. IV DMT

**Design**: Within-subjects comparison of ayahuasca and IV DMT (n=20)

**Why essential**: Must explain the anomaly or revise framework

---

## 8. Limitations of This Paper

1. **RSI values are estimates, not calculations**: No raw data was analyzed
2. **Classification is post-hoc**: We saw patterns, then proposed categories
3. **Sample sizes throughout literature are small**: n=13-20 typical
4. **Ayahuasca contradicts the framework**: This is unresolved
5. **No outcome predictions tested**: Clinical utility is speculative
6. **Dose-response not considered**: Classification might reflect dose intensity

---

## 9. Conclusion

We have proposed—not demonstrated—that psychedelics may induce distinct positions on a sleep-wake continuum. DMT shows theta/delta emergence resembling REM sleep. 5-MeO-DMT may show delta dominance resembling NREM (evidence primarily from rodents). Psilocybin and LSD show broadband desynchronization without sleep-state invasion.

**However:**
- The evidence is thin (small samples, no head-to-head comparisons)
- Ayahuasca contradicts the simple framework
- The REM-Similarity Index is untested
- No clinical predictions have been validated

We present this as a hypothesis worth testing, not a framework worth believing. The specific predictions and falsification criteria above should guide future research. If the hypothesis survives testing, it may explain phenomenological differences between compounds and guide clinical selection. If it fails, that failure will itself be informative.

The goal of science is not to confirm our hypotheses but to test them rigorously enough to know if they're wrong.
