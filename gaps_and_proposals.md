# Research Gaps and Proposed Studies

## 1. Critical Data Gaps

### Gap 1: No Head-to-Head EEG Comparison Studies

**The Problem:**
All existing EEG studies examine single compounds. No study has administered DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, psilocybin, and LSD to the same participants using identical EEG methods.

**Impact:**
Cross-study comparisons are confounded by methodological differences (EEG systems, preprocessing, frequency definitions, participant populations).

**Priority:** **CRITICAL**

---

### Gap 2: Limited Human 5-MeO-DMT EEG Data

**The Problem:**
The strongest evidence for 5-MeO-DMT's NREM-like signature comes from rodent LFP studies (Martial et al. 2024). Only one human EEG study exists (2025 Cell Reports), and it lacks phenomenology correlations.

**Impact:**
The classification of 5-MeO-DMT as "NREM-like" remains tentative for humans.

**Priority:** **HIGH**

---

### Gap 3: No Acute EEG → Therapeutic Outcome Studies

**The Problem:**
Skosnik et al. (2023) found theta increases correlated with depression improvement, but this was 2 weeks post-dosing (reflecting neuroplasticity). No study has tested whether *acute* EEG signatures (RSI, theta emergence) predict therapeutic outcomes.

**Impact:**
The hypothesis that REM-similarity predicts therapeutic efficacy is untested.

**Priority:** **HIGH**

---

### Gap 4: Phenomenology-EEG Correlations Incomplete

**The Problem:**
Most studies use standard phenomenology scales (MEQ, ASC, 5D-ASC) that don't specifically measure dream-likeness, REM-like qualities, or formlessness.

**Impact:**
We cannot directly test whether theta emergence predicts dream-like phenomenology.

**Priority:** **MEDIUM-HIGH**

---

### Gap 5: Temporal Dynamics Underexplored

**The Problem:**
Most analyses collapse EEG across the psychedelic experience. Yet phenomenology changes dramatically (come-up, peak, plateau, come-down). IV DMT shows rapid transitions; ayahuasca unfolds over hours.

**Impact:**
A compound might show REM-like signatures at peak but wake-like signatures during plateau.

**Priority:** **MEDIUM**

---

### Gap 6: No Mescaline Modern EEG Studies

**The Problem:**
All mescaline EEG data comes from 1950s-1960s studies (Wikler, Itil) or rodent translational work. No modern human EEG with contemporary methods.

**Impact:**
Mescaline's classification is uncertain.

**Priority:** **LOW** (mescaline rarely used clinically)

---

### Gap 7: Ayahuasca Anomaly Unexplained

**The Problem:**
Ayahuasca (which contains DMT) shows theta *decrease*, not the theta increase seen with IV DMT. This may relate to:
- MAOIs altering the signature
- Oral vs. IV administration dynamics
- Different DMT concentrations/kinetics

**Impact:**
Challenges simple classification of DMT-containing compounds.

**Priority:** **MEDIUM**

---

### Gap 8: REM Sleep Normative Data Not Integrated

**The Problem:**
No study has directly compared psychedelic EEG to simultaneously collected REM sleep EEG in the same individuals.

**Impact:**
RSI calculations rely on literature-derived REM norms, introducing measurement error.

**Priority:** **MEDIUM**

---

### Gap 9: Individual Differences Unexplored

**The Problem:**
Are there responders/non-responders in terms of sleep-state invasion? Do some individuals show REM-like responses to psilocybin while others show wake-like responses?

**Impact:**
May explain variance in therapeutic outcomes.

**Priority:** **MEDIUM**

---

### Gap 10: Source Localization Limited

**The Problem:**
Most EEG studies report sensor-space results. The few MEG studies with source localization (Muthukumaraswamy et al.) focus on DMN regions. Hippocampal theta—critical for REM dreaming—is difficult to localize with scalp EEG.

**Impact:**
Cannot determine if theta emergence is hippocampal (REM-like) or cortical.

**Priority:** **LOW-MEDIUM**

---

## 2. Proposed Studies

### Proposed Study 1: Multi-Compound Head-to-Head Comparison

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, within-subjects (N=30)

**Compounds:**
- IV DMT (0.3 mg/kg)
- Vaporized 5-MeO-DMT (12 mg)
- Oral psilocybin (25 mg)
- Oral LSD (100 μg)
- Placebo

**Measures:**
- High-density EEG (64+ channels) with source localization
- Continuous phenomenology ratings (visual analog scales)
- Dream-likeness scale (modified DQS)
- Formlessness/dissolution scale
- Standard measures (MEQ, 5D-ASC)

**Analysis:**
1. Power spectral analysis (delta, theta, alpha, beta, gamma)
2. RSI/NSI calculation for each compound-participant
3. Correlation: RSI × dream-likeness (hypothesis: r > 0.5)
4. Cluster analysis: Do compounds cluster by RSI or by chemistry?

**Timeline:** 18 months
**Cost:** ~$800,000

---

### Proposed Study 2: 5-MeO-DMT Human EEG with Phenomenology

**Design:** Open-label, single-dose, within-subjects (N=20)

**Compound:** Vaporized 5-MeO-DMT (12 mg)

**Measures:**
- High-density EEG
- Video-recorded phenomenology reports
- Formlessness scale
- Ego Dissolution Inventory
- Mystical Experience Questionnaire

**Analysis:**
1. Confirm delta dominance, theta suppression in humans
2. Correlate NSI with formlessness ratings
3. Compare to DMT data from Timmermann et al.

**Timeline:** 12 months
**Cost:** ~$250,000

---

### Proposed Study 3: Acute EEG as Therapeutic Predictor

**Design:** Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (N=60)

**Compound:** Psilocybin (25 mg) for treatment-resistant depression

**Measures:**
- Acute EEG during session
- Depression scales (HAMD, MADRS) at baseline, 1 week, 4 weeks, 12 weeks

**Analysis:**
1. Calculate acute RSI for each participant
2. Test: Does acute RSI predict depression improvement at 4 weeks?
3. Compare predictive power of RSI vs. mystical experience (MEQ)
4. Explore: Does theta emergence specifically predict outcome?

**Timeline:** 24 months
**Cost:** ~$1,200,000

---

### Proposed Study 4: Naturalistic Sleep EEG Comparison

**Design:** Within-subjects comparison (N=25)

**Protocol:**
1. Night 1: Polysomnography with high-density EEG during natural sleep
2. Session: Psilocybin (25 mg) with high-density EEG
3. Night 2: Post-session polysomnography

**Analysis:**
1. Extract REM and NREM EEG signatures for each participant (personalized norms)
2. Compare psychedelic EEG to participant's own REM/NREM profiles
3. Calculate individualized RSI/NSI
4. Correlate with phenomenology

**Timeline:** 18 months
**Cost:** ~$500,000

---

### Proposed Study 5: Time-Resolved EEG Dynamics

**Design:** Within-subjects, IV DMT administration (N=15)

**Rationale:** IV DMT allows precise timing; experience lasts ~15 minutes with rapid transitions

**Measures:**
- High-density EEG with millisecond resolution
- Continuous phenomenology dial (0-100)
- Post-session narrative interview

**Analysis:**
1. Time-frequency analysis across full session
2. Track RSI dynamically (1-second bins)
3. Correlate instantaneous RSI with phenomenology dial
4. Identify phase transitions (wake → REM-like → wake)

**Timeline:** 12 months
**Cost:** ~$400,000

## 3. Proposed Phenomenology Measures

### Dream-Likeness Questionnaire (DLQ) - Proposed Items

1. "My experience felt like a vivid dream" (0-10)
2. "I felt immersed in another world" (0-10)
3. "The experience had a narrative or story structure" (0-10)
4. "I encountered beings or entities" (0-10)
5. "Images appeared spontaneously, without my control" (0-10)
6. "Time felt distorted as it does in dreams" (0-10)
7. "My experience was formless or without imagery" (0-10) [reverse for REM-likeness]
8. "I was an observer in my experience, not an actor" (0-10)
9. "My experience had emotional intensity like dreams" (0-10)
10. "I forgot where I was, as in deep sleep" (0-10)

### Scoring:
- REM-likeness = mean(items 1-6, 9)
- NREM-likeness = mean(items 7, 10)
- Wake-likeness = 10 - mean(items 1, 2, 7, 10)

## 4. Methodological Recommendations

### EEG Standardization

1. **Frequency bands:**
   - Delta: 0.5-4 Hz
   - Theta: 4-8 Hz
   - Alpha: 8-13 Hz
   - Beta: 13-30 Hz
   - Gamma: 30-100 Hz

2. **Preprocessing:**
   - ICA for artifact removal
   - Notch filter at 50/60 Hz
   - Consistent reference (average or linked mastoids)

3. **Analysis:**
   - Report both absolute and relative power
   - Separate oscillatory and fractal (1/f) components
   - Use same methods across studies

4. **Timing:**
   - Report time post-administration for all measures
   - Distinguish come-up, peak, plateau, come-down

### Sample Size Considerations

Based on effect sizes from existing literature:
- DMT theta emergence: d ≈ 1.0 (large)
- Alpha suppression across compounds: d ≈ 0.8-1.2 (large)
- Phenomenology correlations: r ≈ 0.3-0.5 (medium)

For correlation r = 0.4 with power = 0.80, α = 0.05: N ≈ 46
For between-compound comparison (d = 0.8): N ≈ 26 per group

## 6. What Would Falsify the Hypothesis?

The sleep-wake continuum hypothesis would be falsified if:

1. **Theta emergence does NOT correlate with dream-like phenomenology** across compounds (prediction: r > 0.5; falsified if r < 0.2)

2. **5-MeO-DMT shows theta emergence in humans** (contradicts NREM-like classification)

3. **Psilocybin/LSD show theta emergence comparable to DMT** (challenges class distinction)

4. **RSI has no predictive value for therapeutic outcomes** (model lacks clinical utility)

5. **Unsupervised clustering groups compounds by chemistry, not by RSI** (simpler explanations suffice)

These are genuine predictions, not post-hoc rationalizations. We explicitly state them here so that future research can test and potentially reject the framework.
