# The Sleep-Wake Continuum Hypothesis of Psychedelic Action: A Novel Framework Based on EEG Spectral Signatures

**Target Journal:** Neuroscience of Consciousness

---

## Abstract

Current models of psychedelic action—including the entropic brain hypothesis and REBUS (relaxed beliefs under psychedelics)—focus on receptor mechanisms and global brain dynamics but do not fully account for the striking phenomenological differences between compounds. We propose a novel framework: psychedelics induce hybrid waking-sleep states, with different compounds producing distinct positions along a sleep-wake continuum. We systematically review EEG/MEG studies of psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, ayahuasca, and mescaline, extracting power spectral data across frequency bands. We develop a REM-Similarity Index (RSI) to quantify each compound's resemblance to REM sleep neural signatures. Our analysis suggests three classes: (1) REM-like compounds (DMT) showing theta/delta emergence with alpha suppression and immersive visual phenomenology; (2) NREM-like compounds (5-MeO-DMT) showing delta dominance and formless dissolution; (3) Wake-like compounds (psilocybin, LSD) showing broadband desynchronization without sleep-state invasion. We propose that the degree of theta emergence predicts dream-like phenomenology regardless of compound, and that REM-similarity may predict therapeutic outcomes. This framework generates testable predictions and calls for standardized EEG protocols in psychedelic trials.

**Keywords:** psychedelics, EEG, sleep, REM, theta oscillations, DMT, psilocybin, consciousness

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Psychedelic Renaissance and Its Theoretical Gaps

The past decade has witnessed remarkable progress in psychedelic science, with psilocybin showing efficacy for treatment-resistant depression (Carhart-Harris et al., 2021), DMT demonstrating rapid antidepressant effects (D'Souza et al., 2022), and MDMA receiving breakthrough therapy designation for PTSD. However, our theoretical understanding of *how* these compounds produce their effects—and why different compounds produce qualitatively different experiences—remains incomplete.

### 1.2 Current Theoretical Models

**The Entropic Brain Hypothesis** (Carhart-Harris et al., 2014) proposes that psychedelics increase neural entropy, producing a "primary" state of consciousness with reduced constraints on perception and cognition. While this model accounts for increased signal diversity under psychedelics, it does not explain why DMT produces immersive visual worlds while 5-MeO-DMT produces formless dissolution, despite both increasing entropy.

**REBUS (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics)** (Carhart-Harris & Friston, 2019) extends the entropic model using predictive processing, proposing that psychedelics relax high-level priors, allowing bottom-up information greater influence. This elegantly explains insight and belief revision but again does not differentiate between compounds.

**Receptor-based models** focus on 5-HT2A agonism as the primary mechanism, with downstream effects on glutamate, dopamine, and other systems. While pharmacologically accurate, this approach cannot explain why compounds with similar receptor profiles produce different experiences.

### 1.3 The Sleep Connection: An Overlooked Parallel

A striking but underexplored observation is that psychedelic states share features with sleep states—particularly dreaming. Users of DMT describe "waking dreams" with narrative structure, characters, and immersive environments. The Timmermann et al. (2019) finding that DMT produces theta/delta emergence similar to REM sleep was described as the brain becoming "functionally deafferented" from external input, processing endogenously generated content.

Meanwhile, 5-MeO-DMT produces formless, contentless states of "oceanic boundlessness"—phenomenologically closer to deep sleep or anesthesia than to dreaming. Psilocybin and LSD, by contrast, tend to produce conceptual insights and perceptual distortions while maintaining contact with external reality.

### 1.4 The Sleep-Wake Continuum Hypothesis

We propose that psychedelics work primarily by inducing varying degrees of **sleep-state invasion into waking consciousness**. The specific blend of sleep-like and wake-like neural activity determines both the phenomenological character and therapeutic mechanism of each compound.

This framework makes specific, testable predictions:
1. The degree of theta emergence during psychedelic experience correlates with "dream-like" phenomenology ratings, regardless of compound
2. Compounds can be differentiated by their sleep-wake EEG profile more reliably than by receptor binding alone
3. REM-similarity in acute EEG may predict therapeutic outcome magnitude

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Literature Search Strategy

We searched PubMed, Google Scholar, and preprint servers (bioRxiv, medRxiv) for studies combining:
- Psychedelic compounds: psilocybin, LSD, DMT, 5-MeO-DMT, ayahuasca, mescaline
- Neuroimaging methods: EEG, MEG, with focus on power spectral analysis
- Date range: 1950-2025 (including historical mescaline studies)

Inclusion criteria: Human or rodent studies reporting power spectral changes by frequency band; placebo or baseline-controlled designs preferred.

### 2.2 Data Extraction

For each study, we extracted:
- Power changes in delta (0.5-4 Hz), theta (4-8 Hz), alpha (8-13 Hz), beta (13-30 Hz), gamma (>30 Hz)
- Direction and magnitude of change (where reported)
- Correlations with phenomenology (where reported)
- Therapeutic outcome correlations (where reported)

### 2.3 REM-Similarity Index Development

We developed a composite metric comparing psychedelic EEG signatures to REM sleep:

**Theta/Alpha Ratio (TAR):** REM sleep shows theta dominance (TAR > 1.5); waking shows alpha dominance (TAR < 0.5).

**Delta Suppression Index (DSI):** REM suppresses delta relative to NREM.

**Alpha Suppression Index (ASI):** Both REM and psychedelic states suppress alpha.

**Signal Diversity Index (SDI):** REM maintains high entropy unlike NREM.

Composite RSI = weighted combination normalized to 0-1 scale (see Supplementary Methods).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Compound-by-Compound EEG Profiles

#### 3.1.1 DMT (N,N-Dimethyltryptamine)

The Timmermann et al. (2019) study of IV DMT in 13 participants provides the clearest evidence for sleep-state invasion. Key findings:
- **Alpha and beta power markedly decreased** relative to placebo
- **Delta and theta oscillations emerged at peak effects**, particularly in the oscillatory (vs. fractal) component
- **Theta/delta emergence correlated with eyes-closed visual intensity**
- Signal diversity increased, maintaining wake-like entropy despite sleep-like spectral shifts

The authors explicitly noted: "The emergence of oscillatory activity within the delta and theta frequency bands was found to correlate with the peak of the experience, and particularly its eyes-closed visual component... The emergence of theta/delta rhythmicity combined with the characteristic 'collapse' of alpha/beta rhythmicity under DMT may relate to the 'DMT breakthrough experience.'"

**RSI Estimate: 0.82** (highly REM-like)

#### 3.1.2 5-MeO-DMT

The Martial et al. (2024) rodent study and 2025 human study reveal a strikingly different pattern:
- **Delta power strongly increased**
- **Theta power decreased** (opposite to DMT)
- Neural activity alternated between firing and silence at ~1 Hz, resembling slow-wave sleep
- The authors termed this "paradoxical wakefulness"—SWS-like neural signatures in awake, behaving animals

The 2025 human EEG study confirmed "complex slow waves" with delta dominance, describing: "5-MeO-DMT radically reorganises low-frequency flows of neural activity, causing them to become incoherent, heterogeneous, viscous, fleeting, nonrecurring."

**RSI Estimate: 0.28; NSI (NREM-Similarity): 0.78** (NREM-like)

This maps precisely to phenomenology: 5-MeO-DMT produces formless dissolution without visual content, consistent with NREM's lack of dream imagery.

#### 3.1.3 Psilocybin

The Muthukumaraswamy et al. (2013) MEG study established the canonical psilocybin signature:
- **Broadband power reduction across all frequencies < 50 Hz**
- **Pronounced alpha suppression** (especially in DMN regions)
- **No emergence of theta or delta oscillations**
- High signal diversity

This pattern—desynchronization without sleep-state invasion—is consistent with enhanced waking rather than dream-state induction.

The Skosnik et al. (2023) finding that **theta power doubled 2 weeks post-psilocybin** (correlating with depression improvement) likely reflects neuroplasticity rather than acute state effects.

**RSI Estimate: 0.55** (intermediate, leaning wake-like)

#### 3.1.4 LSD

The Carhart-Harris et al. (2016) multimodal study and Pallavicini et al. (2019) comparison found:
- **Strong alpha suppression** correlating with visual hallucinations
- **Theta and low-alpha decreases** (specific to 5-HT2A agonists vs. ketamine)
- **No delta/theta emergence** despite profound visual effects
- High signal diversity

**RSI Estimate: 0.52** (wake-like)

#### 3.1.5 Ayahuasca

Riba et al. (2002) found a pattern partially consistent with Class 3 but with differences:
- **Theta decrease was most prominent**
- **Delta decreased**
- **Beta increased** (particularly fast beta)
- **40 Hz gamma increased** in some studies

This mixed pattern may reflect ayahuasca's complex pharmacology (DMT + MAOIs) and extended duration.

**RSI Estimate: 0.68** (moderate REM-like, but data conflicts)

#### 3.1.6 Mescaline

Historical studies (Wikler 1954; Itil 1968) and recent translational work (Kozma et al. 2021) show:
- **Broadband desynchronization**
- **Alpha and theta decreased**
- **Beta increased**
- Pattern similar to psilocybin/LSD

**RSI Estimate: 0.45** (wake-like)

### 3.2 Summary Classification

| Class | Compounds | EEG Signature | RSI Range | Phenomenology |
|-------|-----------|---------------|-----------|---------------|
| **1: REM-like** | DMT | Theta/delta emergence, alpha collapse | 0.7-0.9 | Immersive visuals, entities, narrative |
| **2: NREM-like** | 5-MeO-DMT | Delta dominance, theta suppressed | <0.3 | Formless dissolution, ego death without imagery |
| **3: Wake-like** | Psilocybin, LSD, Mescaline | Broadband desync, no theta emergence | 0.4-0.6 | Insights, perceptual distortion, maintained reality contact |

### 3.3 Phenomenology-EEG Correlations

Direct correlations from the literature:
1. **Theta/delta emergence correlates with eyes-closed visual intensity** (DMT; Timmermann 2019)
2. **Alpha suppression correlates with visual hallucinations and ego dissolution** (LSD; Carhart-Harris 2016)
3. **Post-psilocybin theta increase correlates with depression improvement** (Skosnik 2023)

### 3.4 Therapeutic Outcome Correlations

Limited data directly test EEG predictors of outcome:
- Mystical experience (phenomenological measure) predicts psilocybin efficacy for depression (Roseman 2018)
- No study has tested acute RSI as a predictor of outcome

**Gap identified:** The hypothesis that REM-similarity predicts therapeutic outcomes remains untested.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 A New Classification System

The sleep-wake continuum hypothesis offers a novel taxonomy of psychedelic compounds based on neural signatures rather than chemistry. DMT and 5-MeO-DMT, despite being structural analogs, produce opposite EEG profiles—one REM-like, one NREM-like—matching their profoundly different phenomenologies.

This classification may have clinical implications. If REM-like states facilitate trauma processing (as in EMDR and REM sleep's role in emotional memory consolidation), DMT might be particularly suited for PTSD. If NREM-like states promote "reset" or "defragmentation," 5-MeO-DMT might suit existential distress or addiction.

### 4.2 Relation to Existing Theories

The sleep-wake hypothesis **complements rather than replaces** existing models:

- **Entropic brain:** All psychedelics increase entropy, but our framework differentiates by *which type* of high-entropy state (REM-like, NREM-like, enhanced-wake)
- **REBUS:** Relaxed priors may be achieved differently—via dream-like generative models (DMT) vs. formless dissolution (5-MeO-DMT) vs. enhanced cognitive flexibility (psilocybin)
- **5-HT2A mechanisms:** Receptor binding is necessary but not sufficient; downstream effects determine sleep-wake position

### 4.3 Mechanistic Speculation

Why might different 5-HT2A agonists produce different sleep-wake profiles?

1. **Receptor kinetics:** DMT has rapid on/off kinetics; 5-MeO-DMT is ultra-potent with different temporal dynamics
2. **Regional selectivity:** Different cortical and subcortical distributions
3. **Interactions with other systems:** 5-MeO-DMT's effects on thalamocortical circuits may differ from DMT's

### 4.4 Limitations

1. **Heterogeneous methods:** Studies use different EEG systems, preprocessing, and frequency definitions
2. **Limited 5-MeO-DMT human data:** Most evidence is from rodent LFP
3. **Ayahuasca anomalies:** Theta decrease contradicts simple REM-like classification
4. **Temporal dynamics:** Peak effects may differ from plateau; our analysis collapses across time
5. **Individual differences:** Some individuals may show different profiles; genetic factors unknown

### 4.5 Falsifiable Predictions

1. **Theta emergence predicts dream-like phenomenology:** In a multi-compound study with standardized EEG and phenomenology measures, theta/alpha ratio should correlate with dream-likeness ratings (r > 0.5) regardless of compound.

2. **RSI predicts therapeutic outcome:** In clinical trials, individuals with higher acute RSI should show greater symptom reduction for conditions involving trauma/memory (where dreaming may be therapeutic).

3. **5-MeO-DMT shows NREM signatures in humans:** Replication of rodent findings with high-density human EEG.

4. **Compounds cluster by sleep-wake profile:** Unsupervised clustering of EEG features should separate compounds by RSI/NSI better than by chemical class.

## 5. Recommendations for Future Research

### 5.1 Standardized EEG Protocols

We call for:
- Consistent frequency band definitions across studies
- Both absolute and relative power reporting
- Separation of oscillatory and fractal components
- Time-resolved analysis (not just peak/plateau averages)

### 5.2 Integrated Phenomenology

- Administer dream-likeness scales alongside standard measures (MEQ, ASC)
- Collect continuous phenomenology ratings with EEG for correlation

### 5.3 Therapeutic Prediction Studies

- Include acute EEG as a predictor in clinical trials
- Test whether RSI mediates or moderates treatment effects
- Compare compounds head-to-head with standardized methods

---

## 6. Conclusion

We have presented evidence that psychedelics induce states along a sleep-wake continuum, with DMT producing REM-like signatures (theta emergence, immersive visuals), 5-MeO-DMT producing NREM-like signatures (delta dominance, formless dissolution), and psilocybin/LSD producing enhanced-wake signatures (broadband desync, maintained reality contact).

This framework:
- Explains phenomenological differences between compounds
- Generates testable predictions
- May have clinical implications for compound selection
- Calls for standardized EEG protocols in psychedelic research

The goal is not to replace existing theories but to add a dimension: psychedelics may work not by a single mechanism (entropy, relaxed priors, 5-HT2A binding) but by **inducing specific points on a sleep-wake continuum**, each with distinct cognitive and therapeutic properties.
